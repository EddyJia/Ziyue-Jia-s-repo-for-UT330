Reflection on Research Lab

October 12, 2026 · October 7 Research Lab

Last Wednesday, when my partner and I chose our research question, I thought I already knew the answer. The bakery closed too early, so it should stay open later. Looking back, most of the time in the lab was spent on the gradual disintegration of this idea.

The first crack emerged when I was writing the question. My first draft was basically asking people if they were willing to have later hours. It took me a minute to figure out where the problem was. I’m asking people to agree with my point of view. So I rewrote the question as a question about real moments, such as the last time someone wanted to eat bread at night, and then what did they do?

The answer was different from what I had expected. We use artificial intelligence to play the roles of shopkeepers, bakers, counter staff and some customers. The shop owner has tried to stay open until 5 p.m. every Friday, but failed. The baker couldn’t stay any longer because he was going to pick up his daughter. A student said that when she wanted to eat very late at night, she sometimes just meant 5 p.m. Several customers don’t need the entire store to remain open at all. They just want to buy something on their way home. In the end, our answers to the research questions were much smaller than my initial thoughts. We suggest collecting orders that are completed ahead of schedule within a short period of time rather than extending the time.

I must also be honest about the AI part. This allowed me to practice the entire process in one class, but none of these people were real. The shopkeeper and the baker once gave different closing times. For real people, I will continue to follow up. Here, it might just be that the artificial intelligence made a mistake. So I think our answer is a practical result rather than a discovery.

Most of what I took away was about myself. I tend to come in with a solution and seek support. This experiment told me how easy it is to do this without noticing and how several honest questions changed my mind.
