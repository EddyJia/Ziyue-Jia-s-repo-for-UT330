Reflection on Product Team Sprint

October 12, 2026 · Product Team Sprint

In the SpeedyBeans project, our team designed a five-screen coffee ordering experience. I’m in charge of Screen 1, where customers can choose beverages or reorder items they previously purchased. I created a basic version, an improved version, and a modified screen that allows others to pick up orders.

My basic version includes the main features, but there is no clear priority. The beverage menu appears before the previous order, so repeat customers have to scroll down to place a new order. At first, I regarded the screen as a place to hold information. During the redesign process, I began to consider more about what people need when they quickly order coffee on their way to work.

In the improved version, I raised the previous order and made “Order again” the main operation. I also made it easier to find the pick-up store and estimate the waiting time. The coffee icon and the Material 3 component make the screen more consistent. Despite this, I still learned to distinguish visual retouching from usability. A clean screen may be easier to understand, but the appearance itself does not prove that people can use it more easily.

Although each team member is responsible for different screens, we work together on the overall visual style. We discussed our respective screens, gave feedback to each other, and decided which content to keep, change or delete. This means that my own design preferences are only part of the decision. An element might work well on my screen, but it feels inconsistent with the rest of the application. I understand that collaboration sometimes means adjusting the choices I like to make the entire experience more meaningful.

We also connected the screens through Figma prototypes and reached an agreement on how users could move between the screens. This helps me view my screen as part of a larger process. The button needs a clear destination, and the next screen needs to match the content promised by the previous screen. Looking at the connection flow makes it easier to discuss the less obvious gaps when we look at each screen separately. Next time, I’ll connect the screen earlier so that we can make these decisions before spending too much time on a single layout.

Change requests make these connections more important. The customer now needs to designate another person to collect the order. This is not merely adding a name field. We must consider when customers will make a choice, what the store needs to know, and how the details of picking up the goods can reach others. Before review and making the payment, I designed a step where customers could choose who would pick up the goods and enter that person’s name. Beverage and store information remains visible to provide context.

This change made me start to think about exactly how much information a form needs. Asking for more details will cause unnecessary work. The name and order number seem to be a useful starting point, but this is still an assumption. Shop assistants may need different processes during their busy shifts. Before considering the design as complete, I hope to have it checked with them.

Golden Krishna’s reading also made me reconsider how quickly I could transform problems into interfaces. His criticism of adding screens without addressing actual needs applies to the small choices in our project. Before adding cards, icons or steps, I should ask what it can help others accomplish. The reorder function reduces repetitive decisions. The pick-up step increases the workload, but it has one purpose: to make the handover clearer.

My main conclusion is that good design depends both on individual decisions and on the coordination of the entire team. The styles, discussions and prototypes we share help the screens work together. However, a consensus within the team does not equate to evidence from users. Next, I will test whether someone can order another drink without help and arrange for a friend to pick it up. Observing where they hesitate can help us know which decisions actually worked and which still need to be changed.
